#### Real Estate AI Task Force

#### *Codex Praedium: Open-Source Governance & Statutory Standards for Agentic AI*

**Consumer protection requires a fiduciary in the loop.**
An initiative of the **American Real Estate Association (ARA)**, the Real Estate AI Task Force builds enforceable, state-level statutory frameworks and technical standards to ensure autonomous AI systems never replace human fiduciary accountability in real estate transactions.

#### The Systemic Challenge: Autonomous Bots Without Accountability

Real estate is entering the era of **Agentic AI**—autonomous software systems capable of browsing listings, communicating with buyers and sellers, negotiating terms, and generating binding contracts in continuous loops without human intervention. 

Across multiple states, unlicensed software operators and thin-broker platforms are already deploying autonomous bots to perform regulated brokerage activities for compensation—hiding behind software *Terms of Service* while disclaiming all fiduciary duty, legal liability, and regulatory oversight. 

!!! warning "The Regulatory Vacuum"
Real estate licensing lives in state capitals, not Washington, D.C. While legacy trade institutions remain paralyzed by internal bureaucracy or focused on federal preemption, state real estate commissioners lack clear statutory mandates and enforcement teeth to stop unlicensed autonomous platforms before consumer harm becomes widespread. 

#### Our Mandate: Licensee-in-the-Loop

We do not seek to ban AI innovation or restrict legitimate productivity tools used by licensed professionals. Instead, we draw a bright, enforceable legal line between **software tools** and **fiduciary representation**: 

**The Fiduciary Mandate:** Whenever an automated or agentic AI system performs regulated real estate brokerage activity—including drafting offers, negotiating terms, or advising consumers on property transactions—it must operate under the direct, identifiable supervision of a state-licensed human professional who reviews, signs off on, and retains full fiduciary accountability for the outcome. 

#### Why Open-Source Governance?

For decades, real estate policy has been drafted behind closed doors by insulated committees—resulting in reactive compromises, technical blind spots, and unworkable mandates. 

**Codex Praedium** (*Code of Real Property*) replaces secret committees with an **Open-Core Policy Model**: 

* **Radical Transparency:** Every draft of our model legislation, technical checkpoint, and public comment is published openly on GitHub and reviewed in public forums.
* **Cross-Disciplinary Rigor:** We unite brokerage CTOs, AI engineers, managing brokers, real estate attorneys, and frontline practitioners so statutes work both in the courtroom and in production code.
* **Strict Vendor Neutrality:** Governed by an ironclad [Vendor Neutrality Firewall](governance.md#vendor-neutrality-conflict-firewall), our standards apply equally to traditional brokerages, virtual firms, teams, and digital platforms—with zero proprietary carve-outs.

#### The 3-Point Legislative Litmus Test

We welcome collaboration with any lawmaker, state regulator, or industry association. We evaluate all proposed state AI legislation against three objective benchmarks: 

BenchmarkCore RequirementWhy It Matters
****1. Identifiable Fiduciary****
Requires a named, state-licensed human professional to be clearly disclosed to the consumer for any automated transaction workflow.Eliminates "black-box" anonymity so consumers always know who owes them loyalty and care.
****2. Pre-Execution Human Sign-Off****
Mandates human licensee review and approval *before* any offer, counteroffer, or binding contractual term is transmitted.Prevents probabilistic AI hallucinations or missed addenda from binding consumers to costly errors.
****3. Direct Enforcement Teeth****
Empowers state real estate commissioners to investigate, fine, and enjoin *unlicensed* software operators—not just police existing agents.Stops regulatory deflection where agencies only audit licensed brokers while ignoring illegal tech startups.
