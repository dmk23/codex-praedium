# Model Legislation: The Fiduciary AI Accountability Act

## Statutory Purpose & Legislative Intent

State real estate licensing laws exist to protect the public from fraud, misrepresentation, and unvetted financial risk during the largest transaction of a consumer's life. While Artificial Intelligence offers powerful productivity benefits for licensed professionals, autonomous **Agentic AI** systems are increasingly deployed by unlicensed software operators and minimally supervised platforms to perform regulated brokerage activities for compensation.

**The Fiduciary AI Accountability Act** establishes a clear, technology-neutral statutory boundary: software may assist in a transaction, but it may never replace a state-licensed human fiduciary.

## Core Statutory Pillars

### 1. Prohibition on Unlicensed Autonomous Brokerage

- **Bright-Line Rule:** No person, corporation, or software platform may deploy an autonomous or semi-autonomous AI system to perform regulated real estate brokerage activities—including property valuation advice, offer generation, contract negotiation, or buyer/seller representation—for compensation without holding an active state real estate brokerage license.
- **Terms of Service Invalidation:** Software *Terms of Service* or end-user disclaimers cannot waive state licensing requirements, fiduciary duties, or statutory liability when a system performs regulated brokerage acts.

### 2. Mandatory Licensee-in-the-Loop Oversight

- **Identifiable Supervising Licensee:** Every consumer-facing AI workflow performing regulated activities must be explicitly tethered to a named, state-licensed human practitioner whose license number and supervising brokerage are clearly disclosed to the consumer at the outset of interaction.
- **Pre-Execution Human Sign-Off:** Before any AI-generated offer, counteroffer, lease, addendum, or material contractual term is transmitted to a counterparty or binding system, a licensed human professional must review, verify, and execute an auditable sign-off.
- **Non-Delegable Accountability:** The supervising licensee and managing broker retain 100% legal, regulatory, and fiduciary accountability for all representations and contractual terms generated by their authorized AI systems.

### 3. Modernized Broker Supervision Standards

- **Model-Neutral Compliance:** Supervision standards apply equally across all licensed entities—traditional brick-and-mortar brokerages, virtual firms, teams, and digital platforms—eliminating "thin-broker" loopholes where a single license rubber-stamps unmonitored bots.
- **Audit Trail Retention:** Brokerages utilizing AI systems for transaction workflows must maintain immutable, timestamped audit logs capturing AI outputs, human review checkpoints, and final licensee approvals for standard statutory retention periods.

### 4. Direct Regulatory Enforcement Teeth

- **Jurisdiction Over Unlicensed Operators:** Explicitly empowers State Real Estate Commissioners and Attorneys General to investigate, issue cease-and-desist orders, levy civil penalties, and seek injunctive relief against *unlicensed* technology operators engaging in de facto brokerage.
- **Consumer Private Right of Action:** Preserves consumer remedies when unlicensed autonomous platforms cause material financial harm through misrepresentation or contractual error.

## Technical Reference Implementation: Pre-Execution Gate

To ensure statutory definitions align with modern software engineering practices, **Codex Praedium** provides reference validation patterns for brokerage engineering teams. Any automated workflow that prepares binding transaction documents must enforce a deterministic human-in-the-loop gate before external transmission:

```
from dataclasses import dataclass
from datetime import datetime
from typing import Optional

@dataclass
class TransactionArtifact:
    artifact_id: str
    content: str
    generated_by_ai: bool
    assigned_licensee_id: str
    supervising_broker_id: str
    reviewed_by_licensee_id: Optional[str] = None
    approved_at: Optional[datetime] = None

def can_transmit_to_consumer(artifact: TransactionArtifact) -> bool:
    """
    Enforces the Licensee-in-the-Loop pre-execution checkpoint.
    Returns True only when a named licensee has verified the artifact.
    """
    if not artifact.assigned_licensee_id or not artifact.supervising_broker_id:
        raise PermissionError("Missing identifiable fiduciary assignment.")

    if artifact.generated_by_ai:
        if not artifact.reviewed_by_licensee_id or artifact.approved_at is None:
            return False
        if artifact.reviewed_by_licensee_id != artifact.assigned_licensee_id:
            raise PermissionError("Approving licensee must match assigned fiduciary.")

    return True
```

## Evaluating Rival Proposals: The 3-Point Litmus Test

Lawmakers, regulators, and members can benchmark any proposed state AI bill against our three non-negotiable criteria:

1. **Identifiable Fiduciary:** Does the statute require a named, state-licensed human professional to be disclosed to the consumer?
1. **Pre-Execution Human Sign-Off:** Does it mandate human licensee review *before* binding terms or offers are transmitted?
1. **Direct Enforcement Teeth:** Does it empower state commissioners to fine and enjoin *unlicensed* software operators, rather than solely policing existing licensees?
